## Counterfactual legislatures - roadmap ##

This repo has the intent of examining what different countries' legislatures (usually lower house) would look like if they used a different electoral method. For FPTP countries (Canada, USA, UK, France, maybe Australia) the idea would be to show what they would look like under proportional representation. Countries that already have it could be tweaked; changing between D'Hondt and Sainte-Lague, for example. Spain could use autonomous communities instead of provinces as districts, and Brazil could let go of the minimum of 8 and maximum of 70 representatives per district. Thresholds could also be varied. Ideally we would have a function taking a country name, a desired system and election data for a given year and returning the detailed composition of its legislature. [Examples to add: Germany with only Erst- or Zweitstimme, before switching to proportional-past-the-post, and also what it would look like if it hadn't]

For a case like "proportional UK", for example, districts could be built on a constituent country basis, with England possibly split into its regions. Candidates from each region would be a list, with vote percentages in each constituency used to allocate party seats to individual candidates. [Inspo: the guys on Bluesky that compare the number of votes represented by Ayes and Nays at Westminster] [Also: an "index of representation" - what fraction of all voters voted nominally for the candidates that end up elected?]

This project explicitly acknowledges that it cannot account for the changes in party and voter behavior that would arise from the hypothesis of different voting methods, or the political feasibility of the changes.

A later phase of the project involves examining the impact of legislature changes on policy and its downstream consequences. There are two sources for that; one is policy-to-effect and the other effect-to-policy. The former would look at concrete cases of relevant political votes that happened in the real-world versions of these legislatures, identifying the ones where the counterfactual result of the vote is different, and then estimating the impact of the (non) adoption of the policy in question. The latter would search the literature for instances of the class "when policy A is implemented, variable X changes by this much", and then look for examples of such policies being voted on in the legislatures in question.

A preliminary phase involves building up-to-date versions of the [Shortest Splitline Algorithm](https://www.rangevoting.org/SplitLR.html) maps for the 2010 and 2020 US Censuses. Hopefully the data can also show how many people change districts between cycles (although the question of what constitutes the "same" district is not obvious).

This and potentially other districting algorithms might be used to create single-member maps for countries currently using PR. Denmark has its nomination districts for that.
