# Brazil: the Chamber without its floor and ceiling

Generated by `brazil/tools/chamber.py`. Raw numbers in `chamber.json`.

## The question

Article 45 of the 1988 Constitution makes Chamber seats proportional to state
population and then contradicts itself: **no state may have fewer than 8 or
more than 70**. This holds everything else fixed -- the same votes, the same
513 seats, the same within-state allocation rule -- and changes only that: a
floor of 1, no ceiling. A third scenario pools the country into a single
513-seat national district.

Seats are apportioned on the most recent census before each election (1991 ->
1994/1998; 2000 -> 2002/2006; 2010 -> 2010/2014/2018; 2022 -> 2022), which is
how apportionment normally works and avoids putting an interpolated population
series inside the comparison.

Ideal population per seat: **395,868**.

**The worst-to-best ratio falls from 7.97x to 1.30x.** Sao Paulo goes from
70 seats to 112; Roraima, Amapa and Acre go from 8 to 2. Under the current
rule a voter in Roraima carries about 8 times the weight of a voter in Sao
Paulo. Under a floor of 1 and no ceiling, essentially nobody is more than 30%
away from the national average.

The residual 1.30x is not a defect: with 27 units and 513 seats, integer
rounding alone leaves small states a little over- or under-represented, and
the floor of 1 binds nowhere -- the smallest state still earns 2 seats on
population.

## 2. What the limits do to the parties -- much less than you would expect

| Year | Gallagher, actual | No limits | National district |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1994 | 4.20 | 3.55 | 0.46 |
| 2002 | 3.54 | 2.91 | 0.94 |
| 2006 | 4.94 | 4.49 | 0.87 |
| 2010 | 5.09 | 4.35 | 0.68 |
| 2014 | 4.62 | 4.32 | 0.64 |
| 2018 | 3.75 | 3.39 | 0.45 |
| 2022 | 4.10 | 3.94 | 0.69 |
| **mean** | **4.32** | **3.85** | **0.68** |

The Gallagher index measures the gap between vote shares and seat shares;
lower is more proportional.

**Removing the floor and ceiling cuts disproportionality by only 11%. Pooling
the country into one district cuts it by 84%.**

This is the finding worth arguing about, because it separates two things that
are routinely conflated. Brazil's apportionment is an enormous violation of
one-person-one-vote -- an 8x weight ratio between citizens is hard to defend on
any theory -- and it is nonetheless only a **minor** source of partisan
disproportionality. The parties over-represented in Roraima are not a single
bloc, and the states that gain and lose largely cancel out at party level.

What does drive disproportionality is **district magnitude**. Fourteen of the
27 units elect 10 members or fewer, and an electoral quotient in an 8-seat
district is 12.5% of the vote -- a threshold far above anything Brazil imposes
explicitly. Small parties are excluded by arithmetic, not by rule. Pooling the
country removes that, and the index collapses.

So the honest summary: **the floor and ceiling are an equality-of-voters
problem, not a proportionality problem.** They should be argued about on that
ground, and anyone claiming the cap on Sao Paulo distorts the party system
has the numbers against them.

## 3. Which parties move

Largest shifts, actual -> no limits, with the national-district column for
comparison:

**1994** (census 1991) — PFL 86→79 (-7, natl 72); PT 40→47 (+7, natl 49); PTB 31→26 (-5, natl 29); PSDB 67→72 (+5, natl 71); PPR 54→50 (-4, natl 51); PL 15→19 (+4, natl 19)

**2002** (census 2000) — PFL 87→82 (-5, natl 73); PPB 41→37 (-4, natl 41); PTB 21→24 (+3, natl 25); PMDB 77→74 (-3, natl 73); PSDB 78→81 (+3, natl 75); PRONA 6→9 (+3, natl 11)

**2006** (census 2000) — PSDB 68→74 (+6, natl 69); PMDB 102→97 (-5, natl 79); PV 12→15 (+3, natl 17); PFL 71→69 (-2, natl 59); PL 21→19 (-2, natl 24); PP 33→35 (+2, natl 39)

**2018** (census 2010) — PSC 11→7 (-4, natl 9); PSL 60→64 (+4, natl 60); MDB 35→32 (-3, natl 29); PP 43→40 (-3, natl 29); PSOL 10→12 (+2, natl 15); NOVO 9→11 (+2, natl 14)

**2022** (census 2022) — PSOL 12→16 (+4, natl 19); UNIÃO 60→56 (-4, natl 50); PT 75→79 (+4, natl 59); MDB 40→37 (-3, natl 38); PTB 1→3 (+2, natl 6); PP 46→44 (-2, natl 42)

Two consistent patterns across seven elections:

- **The losers are the big centre-right federative parties** -- PFL/DEM/UNIAO,
  PMDB/MDB, PP -- which are strongest in the over-represented North and
  Northeast. They lose 3-8 seats each, every time.
- **The winners are the more urban, Southeast-based parties** -- PT, PSDB,
  PSOL, PV. The effect is real and consistent but small, 2-7 seats.

The national-district column tells a different and larger story: it
**shrinks the largest parties substantially** (PMDB 102 -> 79 in 2006; PT 99 ->
83 in 2010) and lets several more parties in. Low-magnitude districts
over-reward whoever is locally largest, and that is a bigger distortion than
the apportionment.

## Data, and what is missing

| | |
|---|---|
| Votes | TSE `votacao_partido_munzona`, by party, municipality and zone |
| Population | IBGE censuses 1991, 2000, 2010, 2022, via the SIDRA API |
| Elections covered | 1994, 2002, 2006, 2010, 2014, 2018, 2022 |
| **Missing** | **1990 and 1998** |

TSE blocks automated clients at its CDN exactly as census.gov does, so the
files are replayed from the Wayback Machine with the `id_` suffix -- the same
mechanism `usa/tools/fetch_census.py` uses. **1990 is not in TSE's electronic
repository at all** (it begins in 1994), and **1998 is absent from both the
live CDN path and the Archive** -- checked by prefix search across the whole
`odsele` tree, not just the expected filename. The 1990 election also used a
503-seat Chamber with a different state distribution, so it needs a separate
apportionment table as well as a separate source. Jairo Nicolau's *Dados
Eleitorais do Brasil* covers 1945-2002 at state level and is the obvious
candidate for both.

## Validation, and one known approximation

Running the *actual* apportionment reproduces the real 2022 Chamber closely
but not exactly: PDT (17), PSB (14), PSOL (12) and REPUBLICANOS (41) come out
exact, while PT gives 75 against a real 68 and PSDB 14 against a real 18.

Those two are precisely the *federacoes* -- PT/PCdoB/PV and PSDB/Cidadania --
which win seats as one entity and then fill them with their highest-polling
candidates regardless of party. Splitting an entity's seats among its member
parties needs candidate-level data we have not downloaded, so the code splits
them by nominal vote share instead. The error is confined to that step:

- **seats per state are exact** -- no coalition assumption enters them;
- **the national-district scenario is exact** -- it runs on parties directly;
- **only the party attribution within a coalition is approximate**, and only
  for elections that had coalitions or federations.

Fixing it means downloading `votacao_candidato_munzona`, which is a larger
file per year and worth doing before any of these party numbers are quoted
as precise.
